## 신규 후보 T5 — 멀티뷰 증거 선택 (VLM QA)

**판정**: The broad claim is taken on all three fronts, and mostly within the last 8 months. The METHOD claim (select rather than accumulate simultaneous views for VLM QA) is taken by SportMV-Agent (arXiv 2607.11844, Jul 2026), which builds a 787-bundle multi-view QA benchmark, identifies view selection as THE bottleneck, and ships an agentic active-view-selection loop — everything Topic A proposes, in sports instead of CCTV; E2-Select and CityLLaVA's best-view preprocessing further chip at it. The PHENOMENON claim (extra redundant views add nothing/hurt) is published at ECCV 2026 by SSMNBench, whose Single-View-Sufficiency vs Multi-View-Necessity split and 'distraction degradation' finding is the lab's +15.2pp/+0pp result in benchmark form, echoed by TriViewBench, V2X-QA, and the multi-image position-bias literature. The TESTBED claim is the weakest: multi-view traffic-incident VQA already has an annual community venue (AI City Challenge Track 2 on WTS, with 3D gaze labels and dozens of teams), so a 250-event corpus cannot headline as a novel resource. What genuinely remains open is narrow: nobody has published a rigorously equivalence-tested QUANTIFICATION of marginal view value on real multi-camera CCTV video (paired bootstrap + TOST across 4 VLMs, yes-bias-controlled), and nobody has a cheap visibility/utility scorer validated against agentic selection with cost accounting in the surveillance domain — SportMV-Agent is expensive and iterative, SSMNBench is static images with no method. A paper framed as 'rigorous measurement of view marginal value + a lightweight predictive view scorer for CCTV, evaluated bias-controlled' is defensible but must position against SportMV-Bench, SSMNBench, and WTS/AICity in the first page or die in review. Realistic tier: WACV, CIKM short/resource track, or EMNLP Findings; CVPR/ACL/EMNLP main or SIGIR full is unrealistic for a 250-event testbed whose headline phenomenon and headline method each have a 2026 prior.

### 관련 논문
- [high] **Beyond the Single Camera: Agentic Multi-View Reasoning in Sports Video Understanding (SportMV-Bench / SportMV-Agent)** (arXiv cs.CV preprint 2026): 787 multi-view video bundles + 2,592 QA from official sports matches; finds MLLMs fail to exploit multi-view input with VIEW SELECTION as the bottleneck, and proposes an agentic iterative active-view-selection loop giving +14.46% over the best MLLM. This is 'select rather than accumulate views for VLM QA' as a method+benchmark, published July 2026 in sports rather than surveillance. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11844]
- [high] **SSMNBench: Diagnosing Image-based Cross-View Human-Object Understanding via Single-View Sufficiency and Multi-View Necessity** (ECCV 2026): 3,300 QA pairs explicitly split into Single-View-Sufficiency vs Multi-View-Necessity; 17 MLLMs show 'distraction degradation' from redundant views and rely on view preference rather than genuine fusion. Publishes the core 'extra simultaneous views add nothing or hurt' phenomenon (on images, no selection method proposed). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25634]
- [high] **The 9th AI City Challenge (Track 2: multi-camera traffic-safety VQA on WTS with 3D gaze)** (ICCV/CVPR workshop series (arXiv report) 2026): An established annual community track doing video question answering on traffic incidents with MULTI-CAMERA (vehicle + overhead CCTV) video, enriched with 3D gaze labels; dozens of teams compete yearly. Occupies exactly the 'multi-view CCTV incident VQA testbed' territory at far larger scale than 250 events. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13564]
- [medium] **CityLLaVA: Efficient Fine-Tuning for VLMs in City Scenario** (CVPR Workshop (AI City Challenge Track 2 winner) 2024): Traffic-safety VQA pipeline whose preprocessing includes 'video best-view selection' among the WTS multi-view streams before feeding the VLM. Crude/heuristic and never measures marginal view value, but it means view selection for traffic-CCTV VQA is not unclaimed territory. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03194]
- [medium] **Multi-view Phase-aware Pedestrian-Vehicle Incident Reasoning (MP-PVIR)** (arXiv preprint 2025): VLM framework that processes synchronized multi-view video of pedestrian-vehicle incidents (Woven Traffic Safety data) through phase segmentation and phase-specific multi-view reasoning, reporting 64.7% QA accuracy. Same domain (multi-view traffic incidents + VLM QA), though fusion-oriented and without single-vs-multi-view analysis. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14120]
- [medium] **WaymoQA: A Multi-View Visual Question Answering Dataset for Safety-Critical Reasoning in Autonomous Driving** (arXiv preprint 2025): 35K QA pairs over synchronized multi-camera driving scenes designed to REQUIRE multi-view integration; shows fine-tuning improves multi-view reasoning. Accumulation-oriented (no selection), driving not surveillance, but a large multi-view VQA dataset that reviewers will compare against. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20022]
- [medium] **EgoExoMem: cross-view memory reasoning over synchronized ego-exo videos (with E2-Select)** (arXiv preprint 2026): Benchmark of 2.6K MCQs over synchronized egocentric+exocentric video documenting view-preference conflicts, plus E2-Select, a training-free budgeted frame/view selection (k-DPP) across asymmetric views. A selection-under-budget method for simultaneous-view video QA already exists. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18734]
- [medium] **TriViewBench: Controlled Complexity Scaling for Multi-View Structural Reasoning in MLLMs** (arXiv cs.CV preprint 2026): Synthetic three-view benchmark (1,923 scenes, 14K QA) showing multi-view input flips failure modes (cross-view identity confusion causes overcounting) and severe degradation; no selection method. Reinforces that 'more views can hurt MLLMs' is already documented, albeit synthetically. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26029]
- [low] **SurveillanceVQA-589K: largest open-ended surveillance video QA benchmark** (arXiv preprint 2025): 589K QA pairs across 12 cognitive question types on surveillance footage; single-camera clips, no multi-view dimension. Takes the generic 'surveillance VQA benchmark' claim but leaves multi-view marginal-value analysis open. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12589]
- [low] **Identifying and Mitigating Position Bias of Multi-image Vision-Language Models** (arXiv preprint (CVPR-track line of work) 2025): Shows multi-image VLM predictions shift significantly with image ordering across open and proprietary models; part of a broader 2025-2026 literature (cross-image information leakage 2508.13744, attention position bias 2605.11591) establishing that multi-image inputs degrade VLMs. Supports rather than scoops the lab's finding, but removes its surprise value. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13792]
- [low] **CVBench: Benchmarking Cross-Video Synergies for Complex Multimodal Reasoning** (arXiv preprint 2025): Diagnostic benchmark for cross-video relational reasoning including multi-camera surveillance scenarios; shows MLLMs struggle to integrate evidence across videos. Adjacent (cross-video, not synchronized same-event views). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19542]
- [low] **Ego3D-Bench / Seeing Across Views / SpatialMosaic / AirCopBench (multi-view spatial-reasoning benchmark cluster)** (arXiv preprints 2025): A dense 2025-2026 cluster of multi-view MLLM spatial-reasoning benchmarks (outdoor ego-centric, robotic scenes, 2M-QA multi-view, multi-drone) all reporting that MLLMs handle multi-view input poorly. None do surveillance-event QA or view-utility scoring, but they crowd the 'multi-view MLLM benchmark' framing. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06266]

## 신규 후보 T4 — 다중 카메라 감시 video-RAG 분리평가 벤치마크

**판정**: The exact combination is NOT taken, but the neighborhood is crowded and the headline must be chosen carefully. ForeSea (ECCV 2026) has already claimed "surveillance video RAG + QA benchmark" branding, and I verified its three load-bearing gaps: single-camera UCF-Crime footage despite multi-camera framing, no decoupled retrieval evaluation (end-task accuracy + tIoU only, no graded qrels), and no spatiotemporal predicates in queries — so a paper leading with "first surveillance video RAG" is dead on arrival, while one leading with "multi-camera archive + metadata predicates + graded-relevance decoupled evaluation + distractor-view and answer-bias controls" is genuinely open; no 2024-2026 paper combines even two of those four axes (V-RAGBench is egocentric, CFVBench is web video, AI City Track 2 is multi-camera VQA without retrieval, SOVABench/re-id lines are retrieval without QA, and MedVidQA/NLPCC corpus-retrieval tracks are medical and must be cited as decoupling precedent). On scale: 250 events is fatal framed as a VQA dataset (SurveillanceVQA-589K, 85K TUMTraffic pairs) but defensible framed as an IR test collection — ForeSeaQA itself is 1,041 questions and TREC tracks run on 50-100 topics with deep qrels, so SIGIR/CIKM resource track is the natural fit and the realistic tier, provided the retrieval archive is thousands of clips (the three CCTV corpora must supply distractor mass, not just the 250 events) and every query has graded multi-view qrels. Minimum expansion for that tier: roughly 800-1,500 queries with graded relevance across at least two of the three corpora, an archive of at least 5-10K clips, 4+ VLMs crossed with 3+ retrievers, and the bias-controlled 3-choice protocol plus the selection-beats-accumulation result folded in as evaluation findings — the lab's TOST/FDR/judge-panel methodology is a real differentiator there. LREC 2026 is comfortably reachable at similar scale. NeurIPS D&B is uphill: reviewers will demand either substantially more events (roughly 3x, or a second domain) or an irreplaceable-data argument (real multi-camera CCTV with true spatiotemporal metadata is rare and privacy-hard, which helps), and the AI City/WTS line means "multi-view incident QA" alone impresses no one. Final risk: ForeSea v2 or a challenge iteration could add a retrieval track at any time, so this window is 6-12 months — submit to SIGIR 2027 resource or LREC 2026 rather than waiting to polish for D&B.

### [verified] Q1(a): HCBGen does NOT validate its hardness-matched proxies against natural workloads. Its only fidelity experiment (RQ4, §5.4) validates MATCH-PDF proxies against four SEMI-REAL workloads (arxiv, LAION1M, tripclick, yfcc) whose labels/queries are themselves synthesized or sampled by prior work, and the recall–QPS fidelity check covers only 2 of its 7 strategies (Post-Filtering and RWalks).
- 근거: §5.4: 'they bridge research benchmarks and real-world hybrid workloads. Because such workloads are rarely released or standardized, we conduct a fidelity experiment by emulating the query workloads of four semi-real datasets.' §5.1: 'four semi-real datasets, typically constructed by augmenting a vector-only dataset with synthesized labels, used in prior work (arxiv, LAION1M, tripclick, and yfcc)'. Fig. 3 caption: 'Semi-real workloads are constructed by taking a vector-only dataset and synthesizing labels, attributes, and filtered queries.' Fig. 8 caption: 'Recall–QPS trade-offs on four semi-real datasets for Post-Filtering and RWalks, comparing the original workload with two MATCH-PDF workloads... The generated workloads largely preserve the performance trends.' Fig. 7 compares hardness distributions via 'Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic' and '1-Wasserstein distance'. No method-RANKING fidelity test across the full method suite, and no natural predicate workload appears anywhere in the paper. Note the extrapolated claim: 'These results show that our approach can generate privacy-preserving proxy benchmarks that retain the performance-critical properties of real-world hybrid workloads' — asserted from semi-real evidence only.
- 출처: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.14193v1, §§5.1, 5.4, Figs. 3, 7, 8

### [verified] Q1(b): HCBGen evaluates seven strategies — FAISS-based Post-Filtering, FAISS-based Pre-Filtering, and five research hybrid indices (NHQ, ACORN, UNG, RWalks, Filtered-DiskANN/FDANN). NO production engines (Milvus/pgvector cited only), NO SIEVE, no explicit ACORN-gamma robust-path distinction, no 2025-26 robust paths; categorical label=value predicates only (complex predicates tested only via HNSW post-filtering on SIFT100K in §3.4.4).
- 근거: §3.4.1: 'We evaluate alignment under seven representative hybrid query processing strategies: FAISS[22]-based Post-Filtering and Pre-Filtering, and five hybrid-native indices (NHQ [39], ACORN [45], UNG [6], RWalks [38], and FDANN [16])'. Fig. 3 legend lists exactly these seven. §4.1.1: 'it generates predicates composed of one or more label = value conditions.' §3.4.4: 'Since no existing hybrid index directly supports this predicate set, we use HNSW-based post-filtering as the representative execution strategy.' ### [verified] Q1(c): HCBGen only CONTROLS predicate-embedding dependence synthetically (DLC as a synthesis knob, values 0.0/0.5/1.0/mix in Table 1) and measures query-vector correlation (QVC) as a baseline hardness proxy on its synthetic/semi-real workloads; it never measures naturally-occurring GLS-like dependence in real predicate workloads (none exist in the paper).
- 근거: §4.1.1 Generate mode: 'our default synthesizer partitions the vector space into clusters and assigns attribute values in a DLC-controlled manner'. §3.4.3: 'The baseline query–vector correlation (QVC) becomes informative only when strong positive data–label correlation (DLC) is explicitly enforced in the synthetic construction (e.g., SIFT1M-10, -11, -12, GIST1M-4, and GloVe1M-4), a condition that is neither universal nor guaranteed in realistic workloads; outside such regimes, QVC provides limited explanatory power.' **판정**: No — the candidate study's central experiment has not been run by either team, and both papers in fact presuppose the exact assumption the candidate would test. HCBGen validates its hardness-matched proxies only against SEMI-REAL workloads (arxiv/LAION1M/tripclick/yfcc with synthesized labels and generator-sampled queries), checks recall–QPS fidelity for just 2 of its 7 strategies (Post-Filtering, RWalks), evaluates only research indices (FAISS pre/post-filter, NHQ, ACORN, UNG, RWalks, FDANN — no production engines, no SIEVE, no ACORN-gamma robust path), and explicitly declares natural workloads out of reach ("rarely shareable", "rarely released or standardized") — yet still concludes its proxies "retain the performance-critical properties of real-world hybrid workloads", an extrapolation from semi-real evidence that the candidate study would directly test. VecBench never validates against natural workloads at all: its correlation-controlled queries are compared only to random filter generation and to BigVectorBench, its correlation targets are experimenter-set levels (0/0.5/1) rather than naturally occurring distributions, and even its motivating 3000-query analysis is synthetically generated. Neither paper contains the candidate's condition (a) — real predicates as they naturally occur on public corpora with real metadata — as a ground-truth arm. Two caveats for positioning: the weaker claim that "rankings depend on workload construction" is already established by both papers (HCBGen across label-synthesis policies, VecBench across correlation levels), so the candidate's novelty must rest specifically on natural workloads as the external-validity anchor and on the natural-vs-random-vs-hardness-matched three-way design; and the union of the two papers' method suites (HCBGen's research indices + VecBench's four production engines with robust paths) is precisely the coverage the candidate proposes, meaning the candidate would also be the first to run BOTH families under a common workload. Finally, HCBGen's own related-work section concedes the gap ("the representativeness of those queries remains unclear" for prior semi-real datasets), which is close to an explicit invitation for the candidate study; the main threat is not prior work but feasibility — both teams argue true natural query logs are unshareable, so the candidate must defend "real predicates from public metadata" as a valid stand-in for natural workloads.
